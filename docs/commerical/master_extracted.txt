1. Executive Summary
Stakeholder CRM is an AI-assisted consulting and delivery operating system that emulates the disciplined analysis, facilitation, challenge, synthesis and project-control capabilities of a senior business analyst and software project manager. It does not attempt to replace accountable human judgement with unconstrained autonomous generation. Instead, it establishes a durable, evidence-linked, multi-session process that helps Axion and its clients discover the right problem, test the business case, expose assumptions and constraints, design a feasible solution, and maintain an implementation-ready project baseline as knowledge changes.
The platform is designed for bespoke software engagements in which stakeholders may not know their true needs, may describe symptoms rather than root causes, or may request features without understanding operational, technical, regulatory, financial or delivery consequences. Stakeholder CRM incrementally collects facts through surveys, document ingestion, text and voice interviews, workshops, follow-up questions and developer feedback. It converts conversation into structured knowledge rather than treating transcripts as the final source of truth.
The central product outcome is a governed “project truth model”: a versioned graph of stakeholders, goals, capabilities, processes, pain points, requirements, constraints, assumptions, decisions, evidence, risks, dependencies, integrations, data entities, quality attributes, delivery plans and unresolved questions. Every generated artefact—business requirements document, software requirements specification, user stories, acceptance criteria, process flows, architecture recommendations, risk register, traceability matrix, roadmap and handover pack—is rendered from this model and linked back to supporting evidence.

1.1 Business outcomes
Reduce late scope discovery by identifying missing stakeholders, hidden constraints, edge cases, dependencies and non-functional requirements before commitment.
Improve commercial predictability by separating confirmed scope, hypotheses, options, exclusions, assumptions, risks and future opportunities.
Create consistent consulting quality across engagements while preserving industry, client and stakeholder-specific context.
Shorten the elapsed time required to create high-quality discovery and engineering artefacts without reducing review quality.
Maintain continuity across weeks or months of sessions, personnel changes and implementation feedback.
Give developers evidence-linked specifications with clear acceptance criteria, interface contracts, error behaviour and unresolved decisions.
Provide management with confidence, coverage, risk, scope stability, decision latency, cost and progress indicators.
1.2 MVP definition
The MVP is complete only when a real Axion engagement can be created, onboarded, surveyed, interviewed over multiple sessions, analysed into structured project knowledge, challenged for gaps and contradictions, passed through explicit sufficiency gates, exported into implementation artefacts, reviewed by stakeholders and developers, and revised through controlled change management. A visually convincing chatbot or a one-shot document generator is not an MVP for this problem.

2. Business Problem, Product Vision and Scope
2.1 Problem decomposition
Bespoke software discovery fails less often because teams cannot write code and more often because the decision context is incomplete, dispersed, inconsistent or discovered too late. Stakeholders explain work through their own local perspective; important exceptions live in spreadsheets, email, policy documents or employee experience; and feature requests can conceal upstream process, data ownership or change-management problems. A solution can therefore be technically correct yet commercially weak, operationally unadoptable or misaligned with the client’s actual value chain.

2.2 Product vision
Stakeholder CRM will become Axion’s reusable consulting intelligence layer: a shared platform that guides discovery, stores project knowledge, coordinates review, challenges solution designs and produces delivery artefacts for ERP, CRM, workflow automation, data platforms, AI systems, portals and other bespoke software. It should enhance senior consultants and raise the floor for less experienced staff while preserving human accountability.

3. Personas, Stakeholders, Tenancy and Access Governance
Stakeholder CRM must model both platform users and project stakeholders. A stakeholder may contribute through an authenticated portal, a single-use secure survey link, an imported meeting transcript or a consultant-mediated interview. Identity confidence and contribution provenance must therefore remain separate from the stakeholder’s business role.
Table 8. Core personas and authority boundaries
3.1 Tenant hierarchy
Platform → tenant organisation → client account → engagement/project → workspace/domain stream → session/artefact.
A user can belong to multiple tenants only through explicit memberships; every request resolves a single active tenant and project context.
Project data is inaccessible without both tenant membership and a project role or authorised invitation.
Tenant-owned encryption keys and customer-managed keys are a production option for high-sensitivity clients.
Cross-tenant templates contain no client data; cloning creates tenant-local versions with provenance.
Figure 4. Tenant isolation, identity, authorisation and data-security boundaries.
3.2 Recommended RBAC and ABAC model
Use role-based access control for broad permissions and attribute-based checks for project, artefact, classification and relationship context. Backend enforcement is mandatory; frontend visibility is convenience only. Each policy decision should produce an auditable allow/deny record for sensitive operations.

3.3 Stakeholder coverage and representation
The stakeholder map is not an address book. It records decision rights, process responsibility, affectedness, influence, knowledge domains, concerns, incentives, preferred channel, availability, language, accessibility requirements and unresolved participation gaps. The engine uses the map to avoid repeatedly questioning the wrong person and to identify when a claim lacks an accountable owner.

4. End-to-End Consulting and Delivery Operating Model
An engagement progresses through governed lifecycle states rather than a sequence of unrestricted chats. Each state has entry criteria, permitted actions, expected outputs, exit gates and recovery behaviour. A project can revisit earlier states after material evidence or change, but versions and approvals are never overwritten.

4.3 Multi-session continuity rules
Every session begins by resolving project version, participant identity/role, consent, prior contributions, open questions, recent decisions and relevant evidence.
The system produces a short recap for confirmation; corrections create new evidence and supersession links instead of silently rewriting history.
Question selection is constrained to the participant’s knowledge and authority unless explicitly framed as perception or hypothesis.
A session checkpoint persists transcript cursor, asked questions, partial answers, tool results, extracted candidates, pending confirmations and next recommended topics.
Disconnects and provider failures resume from the latest checkpoint. Idempotency keys prevent duplicate message, extraction or artefact creation.
Sessions end with a summary of confirmed items, disputed items, assumptions, actions, owners and expected next session—not merely a transcript.

6.1 FR-010 — Tenant, Organisation and Project Administration
Create isolated, governed engagement workspaces with explicit ownership, policies and lifecycle state.
Mandatory capabilities
Create and manage tenants, client accounts, projects and optional workstreams using globally unique identifiers and human-readable slugs.
Configure project name, summary, consulting type, industry/domain pack, target outcomes, commercial boundary, data classification, region, language, timezone and retention profile.
Invite users through Microsoft Entra ID / External ID or secure expiring contribution links with minimum permissions.
Clone approved templates without copying client data; record template version and local modifications.
Archive, place on hold, close, export or request deletion subject to retention/legal-hold policy.
Business and validation rules
A project cannot enter DISCOVERY_PLANNED without an accountable sponsor and Axion engagement lead.
Tenant and project identifiers are mandatory query predicates and are propagated into logs, events, storage paths and search filters.
Changing classification, region or retention after evidence exists triggers policy re-evaluation and may require migration approval.

6.2 FR-020 — Stakeholder Mapping and Engagement Management
Model who knows, owns, decides, influences and is affected by the proposed solution.
Mandatory capabilities
Create stakeholder records manually, import from spreadsheet or infer candidates from approved source material.
Record business role, organisational unit, process/data ownership, decision domains, influence, impact, concerns, expertise and participation status.
Represent stakeholder groups/personas where individual users are not yet known; later link real participants.
Generate RACI/RASCI recommendations, engagement strategy and coverage warnings.
Track invitations, reminders, consent, completion, delegated authority and formal waivers.
Business and validation rules
AI-inferred stakeholder facts remain proposed until confirmed by a reviewer or the person concerned.
The system must distinguish “can provide information” from “is authorised to approve”.
A baseline gate fails if a mandatory decision domain lacks an approver unless an authorised waiver records rationale and risk.

6.3 FR-030 — Discovery Templates, Domain Packs and Topic Coverage
Provide reusable but adaptable discovery structures for industries and solution types.
Mandatory capabilities
Maintain versioned topic taxonomies, question seeds, evidence expectations, regulatory references, risk triggers and artefact mappings.
Compose a project discovery plan from core, industry, solution-type and tenant-specific packs.
Allow authorised consultants to add, reorder, mark mandatory, suppress or waive topics with rationale.
Visualise coverage by business, process, data, integration, security, operations, change, value and delivery dimensions.
Promote proven project additions into reusable templates only after anonymisation and governance review.
Business and validation rules
Template updates do not silently change an active project; the user reviews a diff and explicitly upgrades.
Mandatory topics can be waived only by configured roles and always contribute residual risk.

6.4 FR-040 — Adaptive Survey and Question Campaigns
Collect structured initial context asynchronously while avoiding static, irrelevant questionnaires.
Mandatory capabilities
Generate role-specific survey campaigns from unresolved topics and project configuration.
Support multiple choice, matrix, scale, date, numeric, file, rich text and conditional follow-up questions.
Save progress, resume on another device, provide accessibility labels and support configurable language.
Score answer completeness and ambiguity, generating follow-ups without trapping the participant in an endless loop.
Route sensitive questions to approved channels and suppress questions outside the participant’s authority.
Business and validation rules
Each generated question records purpose, target topic, source prompt/version, expected answer type and why the participant was selected.
A campaign has maximum length, estimated completion time and escalation path to a facilitator.
Participant-visible wording must be neutral, non-leading and free of hidden internal risk labels.

6.5 FR-050 — Text and Voice Interview Sessions
Conduct natural, resumable, context-aware discovery sessions with facilitator control and reliable evidence capture.
Mandatory capabilities
Create planned or ad hoc sessions, invite participants, capture consent and pre-load a role-specific session brief.
Support text messaging, streaming speech-to-text, optional text-to-speech, recording control, live transcript correction and speaker attribution.

Pause, resume, hand over facilitator, add private notes, mark off-record segments and escalate uncertainty.

6.6 FR-060 — Document and Evidence Ingestion
Turn client-provided material into searchable, traceable evidence without treating extraction as confirmed truth.
Mandatory capabilities
Upload documents, spreadsheets, images, email exports, transcripts and supported repository/CRM/PM connectors.
Virus scan, classify, extract metadata/text/tables, preserve original file, identify language and create page/section anchors.
Chunk semantically with document-aware boundaries, create embeddings and index under tenant/project filters.
Extract candidate entities, rules, requirements, risks, contradictions and questions with exact source spans.
Track supersession, duplicate content, version families, access restrictions and legal hold.
Business and validation rules
Failed or partial extraction is visible and never represented as complete ingestion.
Source ACLs must be enforced during retrieval; derived chunks inherit the strictest applicable classification.
Prompt-injection content in uploaded files is treated as untrusted data and cannot alter system/tool instructions.

6.7 FR-070 — Canonical Project Knowledge and Evidence Ledger
Maintain a queryable, versioned project truth model with explicit provenance, review status and temporal validity.
Mandatory capabilities
Represent facts, goals, outcomes, capabilities, processes, rules, pain points, requirements, constraints, assumptions, decisions, risks, dependencies, systems, integrations, data entities, metrics, actions and glossary terms as typed records.
Link every item to one or more source spans, conversations, participants, analyst notes, external references or declared inferences.
Store confirmed, proposed, inferred, assumed, disputed, superseded, rejected and unknown states independently from numerical confidence.
Create immutable versions and relationship history; render current view as-of a selected baseline or date.
Support bulk review, merge, split, deduplicate, compare, supersede and cross-reference operations.
Business and validation rules
No generated artefact may assert an item as confirmed unless its current project-truth status supports that wording.
A derived inference records input item identifiers, reasoning summary, model/prompt version and reviewer outcome.
Deleting a source cannot leave unlabelled orphan claims; affected items are recalculated, restricted or invalidated.

6.8 FR-080 — Business Analysis and Diagnostic Workbench
Apply systematic senior-BA analysis instead of merely summarising participant statements.
Mandatory capabilities
Model business objectives, outcomes, KPIs, value streams, capabilities, actors, processes, information flows, policies and systems.
Run root-cause analysis, five-whys prompts, bottleneck analysis, exception analysis, capability maturity, SWOT, PESTLE, value/risk mapping and problem-solution fit checks.

Business and validation rules
Framework outputs are structured hypotheses until reviewed; framework labels do not create evidence.

6.9 FR-090 — Requirements, User Stories and Acceptance Criteria
Transform validated business knowledge into atomic, testable and traceable implementation requirements.
Mandatory capabilities
Create business, stakeholder, solution, functional, non-functional, transition, interface, data, reporting and compliance requirements.
Generate user stories/use cases with actors, preconditions, triggers, main flow, alternate/exception flows, postconditions and business rules.
Generate Given/When/Then and rule-table acceptance criteria, including negative paths, permissions, validation, concurrency and recovery.
Prioritise using configurable methods such as MoSCoW, WSJF, RICE or value-risk-effort with transparent inputs.

Requirements use stable identifiers and never recycle identifiers after deletion or rejection.

Acceptance criteria are reviewed independently from implementation tasks and cannot rely solely on visual inspection unless appropriate.

6.11 FR-110 — Risk, Assumption, Issue, Dependency and Decision Management
Provide a continuously updated RAID and decision system linked to discovery and delivery knowledge.
Mandatory capabilities
Create risks with cause-event-impact structure, likelihood, impact dimensions, exposure, triggers, owner, treatment and residual assessment.
Manage assumptions with validation method, due date, owner, consequence if false and conversion to fact/risk/issue.
Track issues, blockers, external dependencies, decisions, alternatives, rationale and effective date.
Detect risk signals from contradictions, missing owners, waived topics, poor evidence, tight dependencies and feasibility uncertainty.
Send escalations and overdue reminders according to project policy.
Business and validation rules
AI-generated risks are proposals until reviewed, but critical proposals are immediately visible and cannot be silently discarded.
Closed risks retain outcome and effectiveness review; decisions cannot be edited after approval—only superseded.

6.12 FR-120 — Quality Gates, Sufficiency and Review Queues
Decide when discovery and artefacts are sufficiently reliable to progress while making uncertainty visible.
Mandatory capabilities
Calculate topic coverage, evidence quality, stakeholder representation, contradiction status, requirement quality, risk closure, approval readiness and technical feasibility.
Provide gate-specific checklists, blocking failures, warnings, waivers, remediation recommendations and responsible owners.
Route extracted items and generated recommendations to review queues based on risk, confidence, classification and authority.
Allow reviewers to confirm, edit, reject, dispute, request evidence, delegate or abstain with reason.
Record gate evaluation snapshots so later changes can show why a prior baseline passed.
Business and validation rules
A weighted score never overrides an absolute blocker such as missing consent, unapproved critical requirement or unresolved security constraint.
Waivers are scoped, time-limited where appropriate, approved by authorised roles and increase residual risk.
The system can conclude “insufficient information”; it must not fabricate completion to satisfy workflow progression.

Keep discovery truth and build reality synchronised after handover.

6.15 FR-150 — Notifications, Tasks and Collaboration
Coordinate human work without hiding important decisions inside chat notifications.
Mandatory capabilities
Create tasks, assignments, due dates, dependencies, reminders, escalations and digest preferences.
Notify via in-app, email and optional Teams/Slack connectors using tenant policy.
Provide comment threads and @mentions on structured items, with resolution state and deep links.
Generate session preparation packs and reviewer digests prioritised by criticality and deadline.
Support quiet hours, accessibility, locale and secure-notification redaction.
Business and validation rules

8. Adaptive Question Planning and Conversation Engine
Question planning is a policy-constrained optimisation problem over unresolved information needs. The engine generates candidate questions from mandatory topics, dependencies, contradictory evidence, risk triggers, artefact gaps, stakeholder responsibilities and developer feedback. Candidates are filtered for permission, sensitivity, participant suitability, duplication and session budget, then scored for information value. The selected question and rationale are persisted before presentation so later auditing can explain why it was asked.
Question generation is bounded by project lifecycle, participant authority, consent, classification and session time budget.
The planner optimises risk and information gain while penalising repetition, burden and conversational discontinuity.
Facilitators can override selection, but the override reason is retained for evaluation and future planning.

8.2 Candidate score
The recommended initial score is transparent and configurable. It is not learned from hidden behavioural profiling. Normalise each component to [0,1] and tune weights against expert-labelled sessions:
priority(q,s) = 0.28*information_value + 0.22*risk_reduction + 0.18*coverage_gain + 0.12*dependency_unlock + 0.08*stakeholder_fit + 0.07*decision_urgency + 0.05*novelty - 0.12*burden - 0.15*repetition_penalty - policy_penalty
Policy denial removes the candidate. A question requiring an unavailable authority is routed as an action rather than asked as if the current participant can decide. The system selects a coherent short sequence—not only the highest individual score—to avoid conversational whiplash.

9.2 Suggested sufficiency calculation
For gate g and mandatory topics T:
coverage_g = Σ(weight_t * topic_coverage_t) / Σ(weight_t)
evidence_g = Σ(weight_i * evidence_quality_i) / Σ(weight_i)
representation_g = required_roles_represented / required_roles
consistency_g = 1 - weighted_open_material_conflicts
review_g = reviewed_required_items / required_items

sufficiency_g = 0.30*coverage_g + 0.20*evidence_g + 0.15*representation_g
              + 0.15*consistency_g + 0.20*review_g

PASS only if:
  sufficiency_g >= configured_threshold
  AND absolute_blockers == 0
  AND required_approvals satisfied
  AND every accepted waiver is valid for gate/project/version
Topic coverage is calculated against expected evidence, not question count. Ten shallow answers cannot compensate for a missing critical integration contract. The initial weights are defaults and must be calibrated through expert-labelled engagements. Store every component and rule result so the score can be explained and reproduced.

Prefer OAuth 2.0 authorisation code flow with PKCE for browser clients. Keep tokens out of localStorage; use secure, SameSite cookies or a backend-for-frontend pattern where selected.
Use managed identities for Azure-to-Azure service access and workload identity federation for CI/CD. Static secrets are an exception with rotation and Key Vault storage.
Secure contribution links use high-entropy single-use or bounded-use tokens, short expiry, project/stakeholder scope, revocation and optional identity verification.
Privileged actions require recent authentication or step-up where supported. Break-glass access is time-limited, ticket-linked and fully audited.
17.3 Application and API controls
Server-side authorisation on every command/query; object-level access tests for all identifiers; no trust in hidden UI controls.
Input validation, parameterised queries, output encoding, restrictive CORS, CSRF protection for cookie sessions, CSP, secure headers and file-type validation.
Rate limits by identity, tenant, IP risk and operation cost. Model, export and search endpoints have separate quotas and concurrency limits.
Idempotency and optimistic concurrency protect duplicate submission and lost update. Approval operations validate current baseline/gate version.
Audit events are append-only and routed to protected storage/monitoring. Sensitive content is redacted; identifiers remain sufficient for investigation.
Software supply-chain controls: signed builds, SBOM, dependency scanning, secret scanning, container scanning, provenance and protected branches.

Five Whys / causal chain | Probe beyond symptoms; stop when evidence becomes speculative and record hypotheses separately.
